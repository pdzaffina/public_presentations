# Building The Advisor: A 10-Minute Exercise

A tight, timed walkthrough for building a working Digital Advisor persona in one sitting. Built for a live workshop where executives build in the room, not offline.

**Total time:** 10 minutes. **Tools needed:** any chat AI with custom instructions (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini) already open before the clock starts.

---

## The Clock

| Time | Step |
| --- | --- |
| 0:00 to 1:00 | Name the stakeholder and the stakes |
| 1:00 to 5:00 | Rapid profile, 4 questions |
| 5:00 to 7:00 | Assemble and load the prompt |
| 7:00 to 9:00 | Test it on a real proposal |
| 9:00 to 10:00 | One-line commitment to update it |

Say the time checkpoints out loud as you run the group through it. The clock is the point: it forces real answers instead of overthinking.

---

## Before the Clock Starts

Every executive should already have:

- One real stakeholder in mind, named, not a category
- A real proposal, memo, or pitch they are actually working on
- Their AI tool open, custom instructions field ready

If they walk in without a real stakeholder and a real document, the exercise will not fit in 10 minutes. Screen for this before you start the clock.

---

## <!--Minute 0 to 1:--> Name the Stakes

Say out loud, everyone answers in their own head or on paper, no discussion:

- Who is the stakeholder?
- What are you pitching or deciding with them, and when?

Do not let this run long. One minute, then move.

---

## <!--Minute 1 to 5:--> Rapid Profile

Four questions only. Skip anything nice-to-have. These four cause the most persona drift if skipped.

| # | Question | Why it matters most |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | How do they decide: fast and gut, or slow and data-driven? Alone, or by consensus? | Sets how the persona should respond, not just what it says |
| 2 | Think of the last time they pushed back on you. What did they actually say? | The single best input for realistic objections |
| 3 | What topic or phrasing puts them on the defensive? | Prevents the persona from being too agreeable |
| 4 | Think of a time you got a real yes from them. What made it land? | Gives the persona a realistic path to "yes," not just friction |

One minute per question. Executives write short phrases, not paragraphs.

---

## <!--Minute 5 to 7:--> Assemble and Load

Hand out this template. Executives fill the four blanks from what they just wrote and paste the whole thing into their tool's custom instructions.

```
You are modeling [STAKEHOLDER] based on my real experience with them.

Decision style: [Q1 answer]
Recent real objection: [Q2 answer]
What puts them on the defensive: [Q3 answer]
What has actually gotten a yes from them: [Q4 answer]

When I bring you a proposal, react the way this person would actually react: their tone, their pace, their real objections, based on what is above. Push back where they would actually push back. Do not be more agreeable than the profile suggests.

If I ask you to break character and explain your reasoning, do so, then resume in character.

If I tell you they reacted differently than you predicted, update immediately.
```

No polishing. First draft goes in as-is.

---

## <!--Minute 7 to 9:--> Test It

Executives paste their real proposal or pitch into the chat and ask the persona to react.

One gut-check question after the first response: **"Does that sound like them?"**

If not, they get one quick fix: reread Q2 or Q3 out loud and sharpen it in the prompt, live. No rebuilding from scratch. There is no time for a second full pass, and a rough-but-real persona beats a polished-but-generic one.

---

## <!--Minute 9 to 10:--> Commit to the Habit

Each executive writes one sentence, out loud or on paper, no AI needed:

**"After my next real conversation with [stakeholder], I will update this persona with what actually happened."**

That commitment is the real deliverable. A persona nobody updates goes stale and starts giving bad rehearsals within a few weeks.

---

## Facilitator Notes

- **If someone finishes early,** have them run one more test message rather than adding more profile detail. Depth comes later; the 10 minutes is for getting a working draft loaded and proven, not for perfecting it.
- **If the persona comes back too agreeable,** it is almost always because Q2 or Q3 got a soft answer. That is the one thing worth a live fix inside the 10 minutes.
- **Do not let anyone build more than one persona in this session.** One real stakeholder, fully rehearsed, beats three shallow ones.
- **Full profile-building** (role, communication style, trust level, and so on) is worth doing later, outside the timed exercise, once the executive has seen the quick version actually work.
